Clinical Overview

Abdominal ultrasound provides high-resolution cross-sectional visualization of solid organs and major vascular structures within the peritoneal and retroperitoneal cavity. It is the primary frontline diagnostic exam for unexplained abdominal pain, abnormal liver function tests, jaundice, and palpable abdominal masses.

What This Examination Evaluates

Liver (Hepatic Parenchyma)

Evaluates hepatic dimensions, diffuse fatty infiltration (steatosis), cirrhosis, surface nodularity, vascular patency (portal and hepatic veins), and identifies focal hepatic lesions or cysts.

Gallbladder & Biliary Tract

Identifies gallstones (cholelithiasis), gallbladder wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid indicative of acute cholecystitis, biliary sludge, and measures the common bile duct (CBD) caliber.

Pancreas & Ductal Architecture

Visualizes the pancreatic head, uncinate process, body, and tail to detect pancreatitis, parenchymal calcifications, ductal dilation, or pancreatic masses.

Spleen & Reticuloendothelial System

Accurately measures splenic bipolar length to assess for splenomegaly, splenic cysts, infarctions, or architectural abnormalities.

Abdominal Aorta

Screening and surveillance of the infrarenal abdominal aorta to measure caliber and rule out abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) or mural thrombus.

Patient Preparation Guidelines

Fasting (NPO) for 6 to 8 hours prior to your examination is strictly required. Do not eat, drink, or chew gum. Fasting ensures optimal distension of the gallbladder and reduces overlying bowel gas for clear visualization of the pancreas and aorta. Small sips of water are permitted for necessary prescription medications.

Doctor Orders & Referrals

Ideal for patients presenting with right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain, epigastric distress, elevated LFTs, or routine screening. STAT phone reports are available for acute surgical findings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find clear answers to common questions about preparing for your exam, referrals, results, and pricing.

No doctor's order is strictly required for personalized health consultations and screening exams. However, if you are experiencing specific clinical symptoms or plan to bill your medical health insurance, an ultrasound requisition from your doctor, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant ensures coverage and diagnostic continuity.

Yes. We work with all major health insurances and Medicare. We also offer affordable, transparent self-pay pricing for patients without insurance or those with high deductibles who prefer an upfront, flat rate without surprise bills.

At Perfect Diagnostic Ultrasound, we understand the importance of affordability when it comes to healthcare services. That's why we offer competitive pricing for our ultrasound packages. Our goal is to provide high-quality ultrasound services at affordable rates, ensuring that everyone has access to essential medical care without financial burden.

The radiologist carefully reviews your images and provides the results within 24-48 hours. The doctor will then discuss them with you. Reports are delivered securely to you and directly to your referring healthcare provider.

Ultrasounds are performed by technicians or sonographers. They are professionally trained to use an ultrasound machine properly and safely. It is important to always have your ultrasound done by a healthcare professional in a medical facility. Every scan is evaluated by expert radiologists with prompt, accurate diagnostic reports.

Ultrasound is generally safe and doesn’t have any known risks. It does not use ionizing radiation (unlike X-rays or CT scans) and works entirely through harmless, high-frequency sound waves.

Preparation depends on the examination type: • Abdominal Ultrasounds: Fast (no food or drink except water) for 8 hours prior so organs and gallbladder are clear. • Pelvic & Early OB Ultrasounds: Drink 32 oz of water 1 hour prior and avoid using the restroom to ensure a full bladder. • Vascular, Echo & Thyroid: No special fasting is typically required.
